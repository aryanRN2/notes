Introduction

Biologically humans are born with only two fears, the fear of loud noises and
fear of falling, rest other fears are a response to external stimuli and taught
by people around us .The fear of reptiles, arachnids and others are some-what
induced in us.
One such fear among people is the fear of Mathematics . The fear of mathematics, when logically analyzed is merely irrational and arise due to misunderstanding and poor knowledge of intricacies and beauty of Mathematics.
In the article, ”The nation that fears Numbers”,” the author provided qualitative diagnosis of India’s Civilizational phobia of Mathematics. This could
also be traced back to the later Vedic period, when females and lower classes
of society were not allowed the privilege of education thus developing lower scientific attitude among people, which further (a possibility) may have lead to
the development of fear of mathematics . As suggested by a scientific study , a
new born inherits their thinking potential from their mother, which explains a
lot. So, the anxiety generated from mathematics is not developed , but passed
on from generations to generations as a non-linear dynamical system. When
a student asks their mentor for a query but are left with a not that satisfactory explanation a psychological statement could be stuck in their head ” Is
Mathematics really that tough”.
This analysis provides us with the fact that phobia of mathematics is not
internally developed but is transmitted from our mentors like teachers, parents,
classmates, peers, seniors and others. The system of mathematical anxiety
has two stable equilibria: a low-mathematics trap (the current state, in which
anxiety self-perpetuates through teacher transmission and parental messaging)
and a high-mathematics attractor (the desired state, in which mathematical
confidence self-reinforces). We characterize the unstable saddle point (the tipping threshold) separating the two basins of teacher transformation, attraction,
and we identify the three intervention classes that can shift the system early

childhood investment, and institutional revaluation across the tipping threshold most efficiently. We derive quantitative targets, timelines, and investment
requirements for each intervention, and construct a composite ”Mathematical
Health Index” (MHI) for India that can be tracked annually and compared internationally. The model predicts that a sustained, correctly sequenced investment
program can move India across the tipping threshold in 12-15 years and achieve
a high-mathematics equilibrium within 25 years — the window required for India to lead rather than follow the mathematics- intensive technologies of the
mid-21st century.

10 concrete action in 10 years
1.1

ASER + Anxiety Survey

• What is proposed?
Add a mathematics anxiety test to the annual ASER education survey.
Measure: fear of maths, confidence, learning level, district-wise performance.
• Why?
Many students fail in maths because of fear and anxiety, not intelligence.
The government needs data to track improvement.
• Goal
Create a baseline measurement for all districts. Use it like a feedback
system to monitor reforms.
• Cost
INR 50 crore (Year 1)

1.2

500 Mathematics Transformation Centres

• What is proposed?
Create 500 teacher-training mathematics centres across India. Each centre
trains about 200 teachers every year.
• Training focus
Conceptual understanding Problem solving Deep mathematical thinking
Residential 6-week programmes
• Why?
Poor maths teaching is one of the biggest causes of weak learning. Better
teachers → better students.
1

• Managed by
University mathematics departments Funded by DST-SERB
• Cost
INR 2,500 crore capital INR 300 crore/year operational

1.3

NCERT Curriculum Rewrite

• What is proposed?
Rewrite Classes 1–5 maths curriculum. Done by mathematicians, not only
administrators.
• Key change
Reduce unnecessary syllabus by 30%. Increase conceptual depth.
• Why?
Current system emphasizes memorization. Students don’t understand
concepts properly.
• Goal
Fewer topics but deeper understanding.
• Cost
INR25 crore

1.4

Diagnostic Assessment System

• What is proposed?
Create annual diagnostic tests for every primary student. Tablet-based +
paper version.
• Purpose
Identify exact conceptual weaknesses.
• Important idea
The report says: “Output is not a grade; it is a repair prescription.”
Meaning: Test should help fix weaknesses, not just give marks.
• Why important?
Traditional exams only rank students. This system would diagnose learning gaps early.
• Cost
INR100 crore development INR30 crore/year operation

Triple DST Mathematics Funding

• What is proposed?
Increase mathematics research funding from: INR120 crore → INR400
crore annually. Fill 4,000 vacant faculty positions.
• Why?
India underinvests in pure mathematics research. Lack of professors and
researchers weakens innovation.
• Goal
Strengthen university mathematics ecosystem.
• Cost
INR280 crore additional/year INR500 crore/year for faculty salaries

“I Am a Maths Person” National Campaign

• What is proposed?
National awareness campaign to change public attitude toward mathematics.
• Main idea
The report says maths anxiety is often socially created: parents say “maths
is hard,” teachers spread fear, society labels children early.
• Campaign target groups
Parents, Teachers, and Students
• Inspired by
“Beti Bachao Beti Padhao”
• Goal
Reduce transmission of maths fear by parents by 30%.
• Cost
INR200 crore

Make quantitative reasoning compulsory for all Class 11–12 students regardless of stream. Includes: logical thinking, statistics, quantitative reasoning.

Overall core Idea of the Document

The author believes India’s mathematics crisis is NOT because students are
incapable. Main problems are: maths anxiety, weak conceptual teaching, rote
learning, poor teacher training, low research funding, social fear of mathematics. The plan tries to solve all of these together: Better teachers Better curriculum Better diagnostics Better research ecosystem Better public attitude toward
maths

To summarize in one line

The thesis proposes a national mathematical renaissance where India treats
mathematics as a strategic national capability, similar to science, technology, or
infrastructure.
