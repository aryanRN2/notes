- $Ker* \le G$

Proof: $e \in Ker* \Rightarrow Ker* \neq \phi$
Let $g_1, g_2 \in Ker*$. Then
$\left. \begin{matrix} g_1 * a = a \\ g_2 * a = a \end{matrix} \right\} \forall a \in A$
$(g_1 g_2) * a = g_1 * (g_2 * a)$
$= g_1 * a$
$= a \quad \forall a \in A$

$\Rightarrow g_1 g_2 \in Ker*$
Also, $g_1 * a = a$
$\Rightarrow g_1^{-1} * (g_1 * a) = g_1^{-1} * a$
$\Rightarrow (g_1^{-1} g_1) * a = g_1^{-1} * a$
$\Rightarrow e * a = g_1^{-1} * a$
$\Rightarrow a = g_1^{-1} * a$
$\Rightarrow g_1^{-1} \in Ker*$
$\therefore Ker* \le G$

- Theorem: If $F$ is the corresponding permutation representation of $*$ then $Ker F = Ker *$

Proof: $F: G \to \text{Sym} A$
$F(g) = \sigma_g$
$\sigma_g(a) = g * a$
$Ker F = \{g \in G \mid F(g) = I_A\}$
$= \{g \in G \mid \sigma_g = \sigma_e\}$
$= \{g \in G \mid \sigma_g(a) = \sigma_e(a), \forall a \in A\}$
$= \{g * a = e * a, a \in A\}$
