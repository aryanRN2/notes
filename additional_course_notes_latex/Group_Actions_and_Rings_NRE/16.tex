First we show that $F$ is a map and for this we have to prove that $\sigma_g$ is a bijective map.
Clearly, $\sigma_g$ is a map.

let $a_1, a_2 \in A$ s.t.
$\sigma_g(a_1) = \sigma_g(a_2)$
$\Rightarrow g * a_1 = g * a_2$
$g^{-1} * (g * a_1) = g^{-1} * (g * a_2)$
$\Rightarrow (g^{-1} g) * a_1 = (g^{-1} g) * a_2 \quad (\text{as } * \text{ is a action})$
$\Rightarrow e * a_1 = e * a_2$
$\Rightarrow a_1 = a_2 \quad (\text{as } * \text{ is an group action})$
$\therefore \sigma_g$ is a one-one map.

let $a \in A$
$\Rightarrow g^{-1} * a \in A$ and
$\sigma_g(g^{-1} * a) = g * (g^{-1} * a)$
$= (g g^{-1}) * a$
$= e * a$
$\sigma_g(g^{-1} * a) = a$
$\therefore \sigma_g$ is a onto map, and hence bijective.
Thus $\sigma_g$ is in $\text{Sym}(A)$ $\&$ hence
$F$ is a map.
let $g_1, g_2 \in G$ then
$F(g_1 g_2) = \sigma_{g_1 g_2}$
$= \sigma_{g_1} \circ \sigma_{g_2}$
$F(g_1 g_2) = F(g_1) \circ F(g_2)$
