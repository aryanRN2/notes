- Proof: Let $F: G \to \text{Sym}(A)$ be a group homomorphism

let for $g \in G$, $F(g) = \sigma_g$
i.e. $\sigma_g : A \to A$ is a bijective map
for $g_1, g_2 \in G$
$F(g_1 g_2) = F(g_1) \circ F(g_2)$
$\sigma_{g_1 g_2} = \sigma_{g_1} \circ \sigma_{g_2}$
Define $*: G \times A \to A$ as
$g * a = \sigma_g(a) \quad \forall g \in G, \forall a \in A$
then $*$ is a map.
For $g_1, g_2 \in G \ \& \ a \in A$
$g_1 * (g_2 * a) = g_1 * (\sigma_{g_2}(a))$
$= \sigma_{g_1}(\sigma_{g_2}(a))$
$= (\sigma_{g_1} \circ \sigma_{g_2})(a)$
$= \sigma_{g_1 g_2}(a)$
$g_1 * (g_2 * a) = (g_1 g_2) * a$
$\left. \begin{matrix} e * a = \sigma_e(a) \\ = I_A(a) \\ e * a = a \end{matrix} \right\} \left\{ \begin{matrix} F(e) = \sigma_e \\ \text{as } F \text{ is group homomorphism} \\ \text{So, } F(e) = I_A \end{matrix} \right\}$

Therefore, $*$ is a group action of $G$ on $A$.

Conversely, $F: G \to \text{Sym}(A)$ as
for $g \in G$
$F(g) = \sigma_g \ \&$
$\sigma_g : A \to A$ is defined as
$\sigma_g(a) = g * a \quad \forall a \in A$
